Read First — Logos in Email

How email logos actually behave.

Hosting the logo at a public URL and referencing it with <img src="https://…"> is the correct, standard way to put a mark in an HTML email — it keeps the message light and lets the image be updated centrally. Two realities to design around:

  • Use PNG, never SVG. Most email clients (Outlook, many mobile clients) do not render SVG. Every asset below is PNG.
  • Remote images are often blocked by default. Outlook desktop and others hide remote images until the recipient clicks "show images." So a hosted logo is the right method, but it is not guaranteed to appear instantly. Always set alt text and explicit width so a blocked or slow image still reads correctly, and pick the variant whose look matches your header background.
  • Always use absolute https://hiveintl.com/… URLs in email — relative paths won't resolve in an inbox.
  • Custom fonts don't load in email. Bebas Neue / DM Sans won't render in most clients; set a system fallback (Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif) in the email's inline CSS.
